# v0.1 public preview: the release bar

Status: draft for the Decider, 2026-10-29. Modelled on the Usable v1 bars (`board/MILESTONE-usable-v1.md`): observable bars, each verified by a test or a recorded run, and a gap table.

## Who it is for and what must be true

**Persona: Dana**, a .NET developer who directs an AI agent and is curious about AI-accountable code (`PERSONAS.md`). The preview is releasable when Dana can install Phrase, follow a getting-started guide, write a small spec-first API, and see three things pay off: **verifiability** (specs become running tests), **preserved intent** (the Phrase source states what each unit must do), and **reviewability** (a change can be checked against the spec and traced to it).

The preview is not about beating plain C#. The head-to-head benchmark stays published as context with its limits stated (`docs/results/`). Principle: release the smallest thing that is honestly useful and honestly described, so early readers learn from it and the claims stay inside the evidence.

## Estimate

Sizes: S about 1 to 2 hours, M about 4 to 8 hours, L about 1 to 2 days of the mob's wall-clock time including review rounds and gates.

Throughput: cards closed per day from the board statuses: 2 (25 Oct), 13 (26 Oct), 36 (27 Oct), 19 (28 Oct), 4 so far on 29 Oct. The early days were mostly small documentation and tooling cards. The compiler, emitter and CLI cards of the last day closed at 5 to 8 per day, including stop-the-line fixes, because each takes an author round, a gate of 10 to 11 minutes, a review and a CI run.

About 25 cards are in the gap (12 S, 8 M, 1 L, plus the open items), which is **about 4 to 8 working days of continuous running**, assuming the owner answers the licence and package-name questions within a day and the Copilot quota resets on schedule (2026-11-01).

**Biggest uncertainty:** bar 5, the evidence cards. Cards 149, 150 and 151 are first-time measurements with unproven designs; their size could grow, and they might report that Phrase does not help on a criterion. The preview publishes whatever they show, with limits. The second uncertainty is bar 3: if store operations need new language constructs, that card is larger than one L.

## One-way doors (the product owner decides)

- **The licence.** The repository is public with no licence today, which means all rights reserved. Choosing one cannot be undone for versions already published under it.
- **The public package name and its owner** on the package feed, and **publishing the first version**: a published version can be unlisted but not removed.
- **The first public claims** (the README text and the results page): once read, they cannot be recalled; they should state evidence and limits only.

Everything else is reversible: each card is small enough to revert, per the working agreement.

## Order (smallest honest path)

Fit around the running queue (173, then 174 and 175): packaging and the guide are independent of the compiler cards and can run in parallel with them; the evidence cards come after 174 so they run against the corpus; the licence question goes to the product owner now because it blocks nothing until the publish step.

# Retro pick proposal (facilitator's synthesis of the seven voices): a task-force declaration and every amendment carry a standing-rules preflight

Card 176 retro, 2026-11-08 UTC (2026-11-07 local). Proposer: the facilitator, from the seven retro voices (docs/retros/card-176-2026-11-07.md; the voices in .mob/records/176/retro-voices.json). The permanent red-first rule held on this card (one red defect, found and fixed before the green).

PICK: a new Experimental entry, "A task-force declaration and every amendment carry a standing-rules preflight". Before a declaration, or an amendment adopted from an outlier's condition, is polled or re-polled, the facilitator checks it against the standing rules the card touches and writes the result into the proposal as a short list: each rule (D137: at most one Pending criterion test at a time and the green's test body unchanged apart from removing Pending; D154 and D157: the gate replays the red on the commit before the first production change and checks the Criterion order in git log; D204: an atomic green carries `Criterion: 1-n` and the red record gives the pre-change commit, the command, the failing test and the expected failure; the Decider protocol's no-new-demand and 30 percent rules) with the line of the proposal that satisfies it, or the reason it does not apply. Every named rule gets its own quoted line (a quoted line that satisfies it, or a quoted statement of the fact that makes it inapplicable); a quoted line that contradicts its rule is a failed preflight and the declaration is not polled; a window whose committed proposal lacks a preflight line for each named rule is unread, like a vote without a cite. A failed declaration is then handled by the original protocol that the product owner directed on D214: the adopted conditions go back to the outliers and the others get one narrow question about the amendment only; a new full Decider is for a proposal dropped by the 30 percent rule or by an absolute No. The patch is .mob/records/176/agreement-amendment.diff (candidate text agreement-candidate.md).
Not changed: every other value, principle, practice, standing rule and Experimental entry. The neighbouring-test rule for a red seat (run the existing tests that assert on the same command or surface before committing a red) is made a line of the QA brief the facilitator writes, which needs no agreement change; the independent re-run question, which run stands, and the Yes-to-No evidence stay with the product owner's open retro questions.

EVIDENCE (card 176): the declaration took three Deciders and about 100 minutes before the first red (card 324: one Decider); each of D212, D213 and D214 failed on a small condition, and the facilitator's own round 1 amendment of D212 (two reds committed first) contradicted D137, which the architect then found; the architect, developer, decider, audit lens and coach named a standing-rules check of the declaration and its amendments, and the product manager, QA and coach a single window with outlier confirmation, as the change that would cut the decision time without losing the safeguard (the architect: "Put D137 (one Pending), the red-record evidence fields, assertion-vs-blocked, and gate replay in the Ready template and a checker."; the developer: the facilitator preflights the declaration against D137, D154 and D157); the audit lens and the coach added that the preflight must be recorded in git, or the team can skip it. The seats' conditions across D212 to D214 were each already an existing rule or a one-line wording (a single Pending test, a gate replay, the green body byte-identical, a separate sign-off), so most rounds restated the agreement.

AMENDMENT AFTER THE PRIMARY WINDOW OF D215 (the outliers' conditions, adopted): the primary window had architect, developer, decider and coach Yes; the audit lens and QA No. The audit lens's condition, adopted: a window whose committed proposal lacks a preflight line for each named rule (D137, D154, D157, D204, no-new-demand, 30 percent) is unread, the same as a missing CITE. QA's condition, adopted: do not poll until every named rule has a quoted proposal line that satisfies it (or a quoted statement of the fact that makes it inapplicable), and a cited line that contradicts that rule is a failed preflight. Each of these two seats is asked only whether its own condition is now met; the other four are asked only whether the amendment changes their vote.

# Retro pick proposal (agile coach seat): mechanical rewrites carry a byte-parity check, and gate runs are isolated with a load line

Card 196 retro, 2026-11-07. Proposer: agile coach seat. For Decider D202. A change to TEAM_WORKING_AGREEMENT.md, not an issue (the product owner's ruling: process improvements are document changes voted by Decider).

Sources: docs/retros/card-196-2026-11-07.md (sections 2, 5 and 6), the card's records under .mob/records/196/ (green-record-1.md, red-record-2.md and red-record-3.md, preship-gate.md, signoff-ctx6.md, gate-pair.md), and the committed patch .mob/records/196/agreement-amendment.diff (candidate text: .mob/records/196/agreement-candidate.md). Voices: five of seven proposed the first entry (architect, QA, developer, audit lens, decider); the product manager and the agile coach proposed the second.

PICK, one patch of three insertions:
P1. A new Experimental entry, "Mechanical rewrites carry a byte-parity check": a card whose green rewrites many files by one rule names a byte-parity checker in its slice; the red commits a test of it (undoing the rewrite on the new bytes reproduces the old bytes for every changed file, exceptions named); the green seat runs it and attaches its output before it claims clean; a count of renames, files or grep hits is not a clean claim.
P2. A new Experimental entry, "Gate and sign-off runs are isolated, and their reports carry a load line": a full gate or sign-off run is not started while a push hook, another full gate or a seat's build is running; every gate and sign-off report names what else ran during each full run; a failure that does not reproduce in isolation is recorded as load-sensitive (with its tracking card) and does not by itself turn SHIP into HOLD; a copy that needs history is a single-branch clone of main.
P3. One line under CI on main: closing an issue changes the generated MOB.md, so MOB.md is regenerated and pushed after a close, and the mob-process run the close triggers is red until that push lands.
Not changed: every other value, principle, practice, standing rule and Experimental entry (reviewed at this retro: kept, no change; nothing is past its trial length). The Copilot ledger row is updated by the facilitator, not by this vote.

EVIDENCE (card 196): the green seat (Copilot, Rung 1) reported clean on counts (127 renames, 27 and 100 files, a clean grep) but had stripped CR bytes from 9 files including a `-text` fixture, swapped the two project GUIDs in Phrase.sln and lost 2 bytes in a csproj; only the facilitator's byte recompute found it. QA's red 3 found one more miss (an escaped-dot regex the rewrite could not reach). The full gate then showed an extra failure (a 20-second timing guard) in three of six runs at the renamed commit, all three overlapping other work, and in none of the four runs with the machine free; the pre-rename commit passed three of three.

HYPOTHESIS P1: a green that must produce the parity output cannot report clean over damaged bytes. METRIC: byte-level defects found after the green seat's clean report, per mechanical-rewrite card (card 196: 3), target 0. COUNTER-METRIC: green-seat minutes (card 196: about 9). TRIAL: the next 3 mechanical-rewrite cards.
HYPOTHESIS P2: isolating full runs and naming overlaps cuts load-confounded findings. METRIC: gate or sign-off reports with a load-confounded finding, per gated card (card 196: 2 of 2), target 0. COUNTER-METRIC: gate wall-clock minutes and the delay of pushes. TRIAL: the next 3 gated cards. Owner for both: agile coach.

User benefit: renames and other bulk rewrites land without silent corruption of the product's fixtures, solution and scripts; and a red gate is more likely to mean a real defect, so cards reach the product owner without rework.

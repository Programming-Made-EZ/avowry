# Retro pick proposal for Decider D210 (card 324 retro): the byte-parity check for mechanical rewrites becomes a permanent practice

Card 324 retro, 2026-11-07. Proposer: the facilitator. For Decider D210, a new Decider after D208 and D209 failed on other parts of the retro pick. A change to TEAM_WORKING_AGREEMENT.md, not an issue (the product owner's ruling: process improvements are document changes voted by Decider). The permanent red-first rule held on this card (no finding).

WHY THIS ALONE: in D208 and D209 no seat objected to this part. QA wrote "P2 is directly testable and supported" and the audit lens wrote "P2 is git-verifiable"; four of the seven retro voices said to adopt it and the others kept it on trial without objection. The gate-preparation rule and the Dimensions of Difficulty matrix are not part of this proposal and return to the next retro with their objections recorded (docs/decisions/process-and-staffing.md D208 and D209, docs/retros/experiments.md).

PICK, one change: the D202 byte-parity check moves from the Experimental section into the permanent Engineering discipline practices, its text unchanged: a card whose green rewrites many files by one rule (a rename, a move, a bulk replace) names in its slice a byte-parity checker; the red commits a test of it (undoing the rewrite on the new bytes reproduces the old bytes, line endings, GUIDs and file sizes included, every exception listed by name); the green seat runs the checker and attaches its output before it claims clean; the facilitator's recompute from the old bytes repeats it; a count of renames, files or grep hits is not a clean claim. The patch is .mob/records/324/agreement-amendment-d210.diff (candidate text: .mob/records/324/agreement-candidate-d210.md); on adoption the practice also goes to the mob-defaults working-agreement template as a follow-through.
Not changed: every other value, principle, practice, standing rule and Experimental entry.

EVIDENCE: the trial ran on the next three mechanical-rewrite cards after card 196, which had three byte-level defects after a green seat's clean report (CR stripped in nine files, swapped solution GUIDs, damaged project bytes). Cards 197, 266 and 324 found no byte-level defect after a clean report. On card 324 the facilitator's recompute of both greens' bytes from the architect's edits script matched exactly (Resolver.cs 42139 bytes and Parser.cs 39272 bytes, LF, ASCII, 19 changed lines), and the independent sign-off reran the diff and the byte sizes and found the same. The cost was small: a recompute script of about 35 lines and a few minutes at each green.

HYPOTHESIS: a green that must produce the parity output cannot report clean over damaged bytes, so the facilitator's recompute finds nothing. METRIC: byte-level defects found after the green seat's clean report, per mechanical-rewrite card (card 196: 3; cards 197, 266, 324: 0), target 0. COUNTER-METRIC: green-seat and facilitator minutes per mechanical-rewrite card, and any card that skips the checker because it looked unnecessary. The permanent practice stays under the ordinary review of the next retro's agreement pass.

User benefit: a rename or a bulk rewrite never ships with damaged line endings, GUIDs or file sizes that a green seat's tidy report hid, so the consumer's build and diagnostics keep working after a mechanical change.

ROOT CAUSE, WITH EVIDENCE (the six cases of the product owner's entry, and card 203's own): in every case a step finished (or failed) while the facilitator's turn had already ended or was waiting on a signal that could not arrive. (1) Card 178, a CI wait that had already passed (about 30 minutes), and (2) before card 178's retro (about 15): a wait armed on the wrong condition or not armed, the facilitator waiting for a notification. (3) After card 179's green, (4) after card 202 closed with the inbox unread, (5) before card 186's next Ready step, (6) after card 203's green push (about 37 minutes: the gate review was running but no record had landed): the turn ended at a step boundary with the next step ready, so the next step waited for a nudge. And card 203's own retro-voices launcher (about 30 minutes): the launcher died on a syntax error within a second, and the monitor armed on its success line ("VOICES DONE") could only expire after its 30-minute cap. Common root: a wait is armed on success only, and a step boundary is treated as a place to stop instead of the place to launch the next step. Evidence that the fix works: from card 203's close-out on, every wait had a monitor that matched success, failure and expiry and a first-output check within 60 seconds (the Muse launch that died on a PATH error was caught at 45 seconds; the retro-voices launcher's missing table entry at its first traceback), and the next step was launched at each boundary: idle minutes on card 361 about 0, Dispatch nudges 0.

PICK: a new Experimental entry "No idle between steps" (the patch): every background wait has an armed monitor whose condition matches success, failure and expiry; a launcher's first output is read within 60 seconds; a wrapper script writes an end flag in a trap; a turn ends only at a card or cycle boundary after the next step has been launched or a monitor armed on the wait that blocks it; every retro reports idle minutes per card and Dispatch nudges per card with the cause of each.
Not changed: every other value, principle, practice, standing rule and entry. The smaller findings are lines of briefs and checklists, needing no agreement change: Muse is never sent a repository-reading brief (two failed launches, about 15 minutes lost; the developer and coach voices); a surviving mutation of the gate review gets a committed test or a recorded waiver (QA and decider voices) and the scanner reads committed blobs (architect and audit-lens voices): both go to the follow-up cards for card 361's test limits; a declared-versus-ran check at the gate (the product manager voice). D222's adoption goes to the next Decider as its own proposal.

HYPOTHESIS: an armed, failure-aware monitor on every wait and a next step launched in the same turn leave no idle gap for a nudge to fill. METRIC: idle minutes per card (card 203: about 30; card 361: about 0) and Dispatch nudges per card (card 203: 1; the product owner counted six on cards 178 to 203), target 0 each. COUNTER-METRIC: a next step started too early and relaunched (card 186: a gate review on a pre-revision tip, about 25 minutes). TRIAL: the next 3 cards counted from D230. Owner: the facilitator.

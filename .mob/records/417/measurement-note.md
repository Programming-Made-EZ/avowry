# Card 417: the measurement and why the card was revised (2026-11-11)

The architect's slice (slice-architect.md) estimated that putting the five classes of the first card into the one "Bench processes" lane would run it for 16 to 27 minutes against the fixed 15 minute ceiling, and that three of the five classes (the Phrase classes) start only the no-build Avowry.Bench tool and cannot collide with the failing test, which fails in the C# arm. It asked for one measurement before the seats were spent. The facilitator took it: a fresh clone at 46f2f30, one full acceptance run alone with a trx logger (`measure-baseline/`): 455 tests passed in 10 minutes 10 seconds; toolchain stamp before and after identical (dotnet 10.0.404, python 3.15.1, SDK folders 10.0.403 and 10.0.404); per-class numbers in class-durations.txt (class names and numbers only; the trx itself stays outside the repository): CSharpBaselineAcceptance 518 s of test time, PhraseExecutedOutcomesAcceptance 444 s, PhraseEndpointExecutedOutcomes 147 s, PrivateHoldoutResults 72 s, against the lane today BenchmarkHarnessAcceptance 217 s, BenchRowsHoldNoFolderPath 72 s, NewBenchRowsCarryNoReply 48 s and the two Restored classes: the five classes would add about 20 minutes of test time to one lane. The timing test (the only test in the project that asserts a wall-clock limit) started at 166 seconds with no other class overlapping it and took 24 seconds for its two arms. Conclusion: the first card could not meet the fixed ceiling and would not have addressed the cause (CPU load from other classes while the timing test runs). The card was revised: the timing test alone runs in an isolated collection (DisableParallelization), with a guard for any class that asserts a wall-clock limit. ready-body-v1.md keeps the first version.
